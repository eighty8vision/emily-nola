Questions

Mediation vs. facilitation?

Mediation + facilitation are similar. They often work in tandem with one another. If you are unsure which your situation falls into, do not worry too much about it. I will help define the boundaries before we work together.

In either situation, I am neutral, work in the interest of all involved, and help create the conditions for the desired outcomes.

Mediation is dispute resolution. It assumes two or more parties in conflict who have opposing interests, views, or priorities. A neutral third party helps manage the conversation and translates between individuals so they can better hear each other, when appropriate, with the goal of guiding them toward an agreement.

Facilitation is process and communication guidance. It assumes two or more parties working toward a shared goal, even if they are struggling to get there. A neutral guide manages the conversation, translates between individuals so they can better hear each other, and helps the parties think clearly, stay on track, and reach an outcome together.

How is this different from therapy?

Therapy works through a clinical lens, often focused on mental health, and is usually structured over a period of time with ongoing sessions.

Mediation and facilitation is centered on a specific situation between people and what it will take to move through it.

Does everyone involved need to agree to participate?

Yes. Anyone can start the process by applying, and everyone who takes part signs an agreement before we begin. Willingness from each person is required.

I will work with one individual, or one half of a dynamic, to provide guidance and perspective on a challenging dynamic. In this case, typically the other parties are unwilling or uninterested in engaging with mediation.

Can just one person work with you?

Yes. I maintain the objectivity and mediation lens to provide guidance and perspective to an individual based on their reporting of a situation. The goal is still moving forward in a way that is best for everyone involved.

Since I do not have access to all parties, my role shifts from mediator to advisor.

Do you guarantee a resolution?

No one honestly can. I guarantee a skilled, neutral process and my full attention.

Where are you located?

I'm based in Minneapolis, MN. I offer in-person, virtual, and hybrid sessions, with virtual often being the preferred option.

For personal mediation, a neutral third space is used when meeting in person.

For organizational work or work involving an attorney or advisor, in-person sessions are preferably held at your existing workplace or office.

What does it cost?

Fees depend on the situation, the number of people involved, and the scope of the work. After reviewing your application, I share fees and terms in writing.

However, I think about cost in terms of more than money. Time, money, energy, and health are all resources, and I encourage my clients to consider the cost of each. I also consider what may be lost or missed by not moving forward, and what those same resources could be put toward instead.

Is what I share kept private?

Yes. The privacy and confidentiality terms are set out in the agreement everyone signs before we start, so each person understands how information will be handled before they share anything.

I understand that I may be trusted with private, sensitive, or proprietary information. I do not share what is discussed or learned through working together outside of the people who have signed the agreement and are participating in the process, except where disclosure is required by law or falls within the specific exceptions outlined in the agreement.
